# 46. BF16 (bfloat16)

**Stage:** 2 / 7 | **Priority:** Must know | **Prerequisites:** [FP32](45_FP32.md)

## In one sentence
BF16 is a 16-bit float with FP32's 8-bit exponent (same range) and only 7 mantissa bits — robust for LLM inference and the likely baseline precision here.

## Plain-English explanation
BF16 is basically FP32 with the bottom 16 bits chopped off. You keep the same range (no overflow surprises), lose precision, and halve memory and bandwidth. Most modern LLMs are trained in BF16, so serving in BF16 reproduces their intended behavior closely.

## Technical depth
- Layout 1/8/7; ~2–3 decimal digits of precision.
- A100 BF16 Tensor Core peak: ~312 TFLOPS dense.
- 9B params × 2 bytes ≈ 18 GB of weights.
- Conversion FP32→BF16 is just rounding the mantissa; no scaling factors needed (unlike FP8/INT).
- vLLM: `--dtype bfloat16` (or `auto`, which uses the checkpoint's `torch_dtype`).
- Accumulation in matmuls is done in FP32.

## Backend-engineer analogy
A lossy-but-safe compression that keeps the "magnitude" of every value perfectly and only drops low-order detail.

## Why it matters for GreenMind
The roadmap identifies BF16 on vLLM as the baseline to establish. Every quantization experiment is compared against BF16 accuracy and energy.

## Common mistakes
- Switching to FP16 thinking it's "the same" — FP16's narrow range can overflow in some activations.
- Assuming the baseline is BF16 without confirming from the briefing/config.
