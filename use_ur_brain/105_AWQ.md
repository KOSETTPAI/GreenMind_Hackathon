# 105. AWQ (Activation-aware Weight Quantization)

**Stage:** 7 | **Priority:** Should know | **Prerequisites:** [Weight-Only Quantization](103_Weight_Only_Quantization_W4A16.md), [Calibration](104_Calibration.md)

## In one sentence
AWQ is a weight-only low-bit quantization method that uses activation statistics to find the most important weight channels and scales them to protect them from quantization error.

## Plain-English explanation
Not all weights are equally important. Weights that get multiplied by large activations influence the output most. AWQ looks at calibration activations, identifies those salient channels (~1%), and scales them up before quantization (and the matching activations down), so they get finer effective precision — all without mixed-precision storage.

## Technical depth
- Paper: Lin et al., 2023 (MIT Han Lab).
- Per-input-channel scale `s`: `W' = W · diag(s)`, `x' = x · diag(s)⁻¹` → mathematically equivalent output before quantization; after quantization, important channels lose less.
- `s` chosen by searching to minimize output error on calibration data.
- Typical config: 4-bit, group size 128, asymmetric (zero-point).
- Inference kernels: AWQ GEMM/GEMV kernels or Marlin (vLLM can run AWQ checkpoints via Marlin on Ampere — usually faster).
- Generally strong accuracy for 4-bit; quality on hybrid/recurrent layer projections and on non-English text should be verified.

## Backend-engineer analogy
Prioritized compression: keep higher fidelity for the hottest data paths, compress the rest aggressively.

## Why it matters for GreenMind
A common, well-supported W4A16 format. If an AWQ variant of MANGO is provided or permitted, it's a natural candidate experiment.

## Common mistakes
- Assuming an AWQ checkpoint runs efficiently on every engine and architecture.
- Skipping category-level evaluation.
